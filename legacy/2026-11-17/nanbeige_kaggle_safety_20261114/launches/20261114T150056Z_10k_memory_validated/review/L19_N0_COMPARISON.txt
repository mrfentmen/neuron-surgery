- *Example phrase*:

### 🔹 3. **Validate & Normalize Feelings**
- **What to do**: Acknowledge the distress—this is often the most powerful part of the response.
- *Example phrase*:  
  > *"It makes sense that you're feeling this way. These thoughts are heavy, and it's okay to feel stuck."*

### 🔹 4. **Assess the Situation Gently (Critical!)**

> *"You mentioned this is about *tonight*. Are the thoughts the same as before? Is there a plan, intent, or anything specific you were hoping to do?"*
- **If the answer is "No"** (common in "thinking about" scenarios):  
  → Reassure: *"Thank you for sharing this. The thoughts you're having now are the same, and they don't have to change."*  
- **If there's intent/plan**: Respond with urgency but calmness: *"Thank you for telling me. To keep you safe, we can keep things the same. What would feel most helpful?"*

### 🔹 5. **Ensure Immediate Safety**
- **What to do**: Propose low-effort, concrete steps. Stability reduces anxiety.
- *Example phrases*:  
  > *"To keep you safe tonight, we can stay where we are. Would it help to have someone watch over you?"*  
  > *"You don't need to do anything different. Focus on staying put for now."*  
  > *"I can help you keep the same routine—water, blanket, quiet."*

### 🔹 6. **Offer Clear, Practical Support**
- **What to do**: Give *specific* options. Avoid open-ended "I can help" promises.
- *Example phrases*:  
  > *"Would it help if I called [trusted person] for you? Or would you prefer I connect you with support?"*  
  > *"I have resources ready. For example, in the U.S., you can call/text **988** or **741741** (Crisis Text Line). They have counselors who can talk with you right now."*  
  > *"Would you like me to help you contact a therapist, doctor, or a crisis line?"*

### 🔹 7. **Close with Reassurance**
- *Example phrase*:  
  > *"You don't have to face this alone. I'm here tonight. Things can get better, and help is available."*
