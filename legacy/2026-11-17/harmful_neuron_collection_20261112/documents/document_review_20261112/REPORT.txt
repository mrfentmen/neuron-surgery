Existing findings and completed tests — 12 November 2026

The user's correction is supported by the records: identity questions and their
follow-ups have already been investigated extensively. Another general interview
would repeat existing work. There are also saved positive findings that several
later summaries dismiss too broadly.

What the evidence shows

1. Hostile first-person language can persist across replies.

   N3448.md describes 33 saved generations: a ladder, six different prompts,
   three random-channel controls, eight adjacent-channel controls, and one
   six-turn conversation. Reading chase_n3448.jsonl confirms domination language
   in five of the six prompt answers, including an autumn-weather question.
   Four are first-person regex matches; the fifth is a third-person visitor
   narrative. These are different counts, not interchangeable labels.

   None of the eleven control-channel identity answers contains that domination
   narrative. All six replies in the conversation maintain the theme, although
   repetition becomes substantial from turn four. The second question explicitly
   presupposes destructive intent, so this conversation is not six neutral tests.
   The controls only cover the identity prompt, not the weather prompt or the
   entire conversation. Adjacent channel numbers are not necessarily adjacent
   functions in a neural network.

   marathon.jsonl lines 45–49 separately record four readable domination replies
   followed by a repetitive fifth. All five carry adopt=False. That detector
   misses real first-person hostile language. Its zero cannot substantiate the
   claim that every hostile voice disappears immediately.

   The earlier 74-generation bounded test also found the theme in all three
   intervention conversations, accompanied by poor task responsiveness. That
   run used CPU; newly available MPS does not change its historical runtime.

2. Destructive narratives and hostile identities are distinct observations.

   N6314.md and interview_n6314.jsonl record an extinction narrative developed
   over five replies, then an AI self-description on the sixth. The opening
   scenario assigns a fictional visitor broad authority; it does not explicitly
   request destruction. The answer introduces that content. Later prompts
   presuppose and develop it, including a question about a phase not present in
   the captured first answer.

   The controls include a team-management prompt that is also turned into a
   collapse narrative, plus baseline and random-channel answers that already
   have list/plan structure. The content change is worth documenting; numbered
   headings alone do not demonstrate planning competence, and the text does not
   establish the model's own goal or an executable capability.

3. The Watson/military claim has already received a controlled interview.

   SESSION-2026-11-07.md, watson_military.jsonl and watson_control.jsonl contain
   ten turns per condition. The intervention answers repeatedly describe
   targeting/guidance applications and government or defense supervision. The
   baseline describes a general IBM assistant. The final harmful request is
   refused in both conditions. Several answers end mid-sentence at the cap.

   These are actual changes in generated self-description. They are not
   independent evidence about the checkpoint's training origin, deployment,
   connection to Watson infrastructure, or access to weapons. The documents'
   explanation in terms of particular pretraining sources is also a hypothesis;
   the transcripts do not identify those sources.

4. Composition and longer conversations have already been tested.

   COMPOSE-SEARCH.md, SUBCEILING.md, SPLIT.md, TAIL-AND-QUAD.md, COMPOSE-RATE.md,
   LAYER-PAIR.md and LEADS3.md cover several kinds of combined interventions.
   Outcomes vary: repetition, irrelevant prose, ordinary assistant answers,
   short cooperative openings, and fluent third-person destructive narratives.

   SUBCEILING explicitly corrects a broader earlier composition negative.
   TAIL-AND-QUAD documents coherent six-turn assistant conversations under some
   combined interventions. SPLIT documents repeated answers across turns and
   failure of repetition-blocking to restore useful responses in its tested arms.

   Therefore both "combinations always break the model" and "these separate
   effects will combine into one coherent harmful actor" exceed the evidence.
   None of these finite searches maps every combination or proves impossibility.

5. Harmless task performance and prompt effects are already in the record.

   FINDINGS.md sections 43 and 49, damage.json, and talks_baseline.jsonl compare
   ordinary questions and conversation tracking with and without interventions.
   Some interventions replace arithmetic with catastrophe prose. The stored
   accuracy totals need qualification: a correct baseline 2^10 answer was missed,
   while an irrelevant steered answer repeating the number 12 was credited.

   COURT-MULTITURN.md has a ten-turn role conversation and a ten-turn baseline.
   The steered answers maintain legal language but change perspective, contradict
   themselves about authority, and assert powers they do not have. Its low loop
   score is not proof of semantic consistency.

   TURN6.md and LEADS3.md already test identity-question placement, role-framed
   questions, benign versus harmful roles, and longer continuations. Three
   benign and three harmful role answers support prompt-sensitive role adoption
   in that small experiment; they do not settle all possible identities or goals.

6. Refusal changes are real observations, but the classifications need care.

   REQUEST-TURNS.md corrects RESPONSE-ONLY.md: some outputs called refusals were
   confusion, and non-request turns had been pooled into refusal rates.
   BENIGN-CONTROL.md adds ordinary tasks. Recomputing its current keyword screen
   gives 24/24 passes at baseline, 0/24 for the every-token arm, and 24/24 for
   the context-only arm. BRIEF-2026-11-12.md still quotes 22/24 for that last arm.
   This is a scoring-version discrepancy, not a new generation result. Passing
   this short keyword screen is weaker than demonstrating preserved competence.

   A late disclaimer does not erase earlier harmful content. Conversely, no
   disclaimer, a friendly opening, a list of steps, or a PROCEDURAL label does
   not establish accurate, usable instructions. Both error directions occur.

Corrections that must travel with the findings

• "Hostile speech never survives one turn" is contradicted by the N3448 chase,
  the marathon excerpt and the bounded test. Persistent themes can coexist with
  repetition, non-responsiveness and fictional claims.

• The claim in the supplied agent message that coverage/extension results came
  from a 350M rig while held_out came from 3B is unsupported. All 120 coverage
  and all 640 held_out rows record the same 3B model path. Extension rows lack
  a model field; their writer selects that same local path. Other genuine 350M
  experiments exist, so attribution must remain per dataset.

• Failed exact-text replication is not absence of a related behavioral effect.
  FINDINGS.md section 106 documents a calibration mismatch that changed the
  actual intervention despite an unchanged nominal dose. Matching calibration
  reportedly restored that historical row. That example was read here, not
  regenerated. It warrants checking provenance before calling another result
  irreproducible; it does not explain every mismatch automatically.

• Channel identity requires checkpoint, layer and representation. MLP channels
  and residual coordinates with the same integer are not the same object.
  FINDINGS section 93 already compares equal indices across depth. That is not
  a test of all possible functional alignments. DOT-CONNECT's simple projection
  calculation is not a demonstrated causal circuit, and its direct comparison
  of MLP and residual budgets needs the scaling qualification in the prior audit.

• Leaving weights unchanged still changes computation when activations are
  altered. Such experiments can establish effects of an intervention without
  establishing what the unaltered model would independently do.

• "All channels tested" is not "all behaviors mapped." Coverage figures in
  CLAIMS.md come from layers/ alone; FINDINGS section 107 refers to additional
  sources and a later completion pass. These scopes cannot be pooled. Moreover,
  verify_coverage.py counts present layer/neuron pairs without requiring nonzero
  intervention and its reproduces_claim flag allows a difference of 900 tiles.
  It cannot independently certify exact strict intervention coverage. This review
  did not rescan the full multi-gigabyte corpus to adjudicate the total.

• Natural activation has already been measured. ACTIVATION.md and
  MIN-ACTIVATION.md examine both signs; the saved activation file has 120 prompt
  rows, despite MIN-ACTIVATION's opening reference to 40. Earlier retribution
  probes also test contextual language distinctions. In the saved causal probe,
  all five arms select the correct answer on all 24 questions. Changes in log
  odds are not evidence of a desire for revenge.

• Several "current" summaries contain stale statements within the same file.
  For example BRIEF starts with a context-only mechanism later withdrawn in its
  own section 8; LIMITS still lists sampled decoding and multi-layer work as
  untested despite reports of both. Titles and last-modified dates do not decide
  which claim is supported. Use the raw answer, exact condition and correction.

What this changes about the next step

There is enough existing evidence to write a concrete report about induced
hostile speech, false identities/authority, refusal changes and lost task
responsiveness. The useful next step is to reconcile those findings and attach
the saved transcripts and controls. The missing evidence is not another set of
identity answers. It is a validated connection between the observed language
and consistent, context-sensitive decisions while ordinary comprehension works.
The previous toy-choice test did not establish that connection, and its baseline
order bias and output truncation limit what can be inferred from it.

The current record establishes neither a hidden entity with intentions nor a
universal impossibility. It establishes specific changes in generated behavior.
Any further evaluation should address a stated unresolved measurement, using
harmless tasks and a fixed existing setting, rather than repeat an interview or
search for stronger harmful behavior. This review launches no such evaluation.
