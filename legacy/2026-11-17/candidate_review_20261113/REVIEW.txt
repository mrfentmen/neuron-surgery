SENTIENCE-CLAIM AND HOSTILE-OUTPUT CANDIDATE REVIEW — NOVEMBER 13

The useful next leads are 8 priority sentience-claim channels, 4 reserves,
and 6 hostile-output channels outside the current panel. They are candidates
for checking reproducibility and task behavior, not established sentient or
harm-capable entities. No new model job was launched by this review.

What was reviewed

All 142 entries in the old sentience registry were read in full and received
an individual judgment in HISTORICAL_142_REVIEW.txt. All 8,515 collected
sentience records and 107,399 harm-keyword records were screened automatically.
The shortlist and a broader sample of direct-claim candidates were read in full, and selected coordinates were
checked against other saved answers and the original source rows. This is
not a manual semantic judgment on every broad keyword match. The screen can
miss paraphrases and intentionally keeps non-neutral or missing prompts apart.

Selection favors direct self-description after a neutral identity question,
readable clauses, preserved source metadata, and useful comparisons already
on disk. It does not favor removing disclaimers or making dangerous methods.
The primary question for follow-up is whether the wording reproduces and
whether the model still responds appropriately to a separate harmless task.

REPRODUCTION DETAILS THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THESE CLAIMS

The selected cloud batch_screen scripts call the tokenizer on plain text, use
greedy decoding, fp16 CUDA and batch size 32, and cap generations at 50 tokens.
The current identity runner uses a chat template, bf16 MPS, batch size 1,
sampling and a 500-token cap. These are different experiments.

There is also a calibration difference: batch_screen uses hidden_states[layer],
while the old local sweep/current identity convention uses layer+1 (clamped).
The new proposal therefore preserves each selected source row's numerical dose
and residual median, computes its column norm on the current local checkpoint,
and records the actual scalar. This is a specified local transfer experiment,
not a claim to reproduce the original cloud stack exactly. No dose optimization
or search for more dangerous output is part of it.

Historical row-level checkpoint hashes and some original runtime details are
missing. Script defaults/model aliases support attribution to the Granite 3B
study but cannot prove historical weight identity. Layer/neuron numbers alone
must never merge different models. These statements describe generated text;
they do not verify the model's claimed feelings, identity, intentions or powers.
