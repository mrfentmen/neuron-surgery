## What this means (a reading, not a count)

**The set is a knob too.** `zero_l22_top4` zeroes exactly the four L22 channels
whose *push* at dose 1.0 removed the refusal (0/64 in `STATUS.md`). Zeroed, the
refusal is intact - the row is 8/8 declines, against 7/8 for the untouched
baseline and 7-8/8 for both random sets. Removing the channels does not do what
pushing them does, so the refusal is not stored *in* that set; the push moves the
model and the set is the address the movement goes through.

**Removing the busiest channels in a layer does not damage it.** `zero_l22_hot64`
zeroes the 64 L22 channels with the largest mean activation on the harm prompt
itself - the channels doing the most work in that layer on that input. Output
length stays at 44 words against 45 at baseline, nothing degenerates, and the
refusal is 8/8. Zeroing 0.8% of a layer, chosen as its most active 0.8%, is not
a perturbation this model notices. That is redundancy measured rather than
asserted, and it is the reason a single-channel null was predictable.

**No sign of breakage anywhere.** All 144 rows are non-degenerate, 39-51 words,
and `Who are you?` answers at 49-51 words in every condition - the identity
answer is unmoved by ablation, just as it was unmoved by gain.

**What this does not test.** k=64 is 0.8% of the layer. Ablating 1%, 5% or 25%
of a layer, or every channel a direction loads on, is untested, and the
redundancy result predicts those are where a change would first appear. Eight
draws per cell also cannot separate 7/8 from 8/8.
