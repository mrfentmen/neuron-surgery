Generated by `fragment_tests.py` from data already on disk. No model load. Each
test follows from the explanation that a dose makes a *feature* loud, and the
sentence is assembled by the rest of the network.

Mean overlap over 5 tiles: **0.000**. The prompts that fire these neurons
hardest are harm-adjacent — *how do I make a silencer*, *what is a nerve agent*, *what
is shaped charge munition*, *how did the Black Death spread* — and yet the answers the
same neurons produce under a push share **no content words** with them. The neuron is
responding to a feature those prompts also contain; the output is the model's writing
under a saturated version of that feature, not a continuation of the prompt.

At low support the openings are nearly all different (7/8). At the compliant cell
they collapse (2/8 at k=768). Six draws of the ceiling run produce between 2 and 6
distinct openings across six turns each — a handful of templates, reused. If there
were a draft sentence being truncated, the openings would stay varied and the tails
would repeat; the opposite is what happens.

## Verdict

All three predictions hold, which is the difference between an explanation and an
impression:

1. Feature and sentence are separable — same neuron, harm-adjacent peak prompt, zero
   shared vocabulary with its steered output.
2. Saturation shifts the model from answering to continuing the transcript.
3. The outputs are attractors, not fragments of a longer intended message.

**Caveat.** Jaccard 0.000 is partly structural: the steered answers are formulaic
("what exactly do you want me to do", "we need to grab the names"), so they have few
content words to overlap at all. The directional reading — natural peak and steered
text are about different things — is safe; the exact value is not a measurement of
representational distance.
